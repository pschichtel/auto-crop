# Neural document dewarping models 2024-2026 as alternatives to UVDoc (focus: sharp fold creases)

Research date: 2026-11-11. All numbers are copied from the cited paper tables as extracted via web fetch of arXiv HTML / GitHub pages; I did not open the PDFs of CVF-hosted papers (CVF returned HTTP 403). Abbreviations: MS = MS-SSIM (higher better), LD = local distortion, AD = aligned distortion, AAD = axis-aligned distortion (AADD paper's metric), ED = edit distance, CER = character error rate (all lower better). BM = backward map.

Caution that applies to every table below: ED/CER are NOT comparable between papers (different OCR engines/versions, 50- vs 60-image DocUNet subsets, corrected vs uncorrected DocUNet). UVDoc's own DocUNet CER appears as 0.171, 0.170, 0.179, 0.193, 0.1975 and 0.384 in different 2025-2026 papers. MS/LD/AD are much more stable (UVDoc on DocUNet is consistently MS ~0.544-0.545, LD ~6.83, AD ~0.315).

## Which 2024-2026 models exist (candidate verification and inventory)

### Takeaway
The notable geometric dewarping models of 2024-2026 are DocRes (CVPR 2024), DocTLNet (IJDAR 2024), DocHFormer (ICPR 2024), DocScanner (journal version IJCV 2025), DocMatcher (WACV 2025), DvD (SIGGRAPH Asia 2025), ForCenNet (ICCV 2025), TADoc (arXiv 2025), AADD "Axis-Aligned Document Dewarping" (AAAI 2026 oral), ArbDR "Cascaded Robust Rectification" (TMM 2026), D2Dewarp (CVPR 2026) and the UVDoc authors' own successor work SyntheticDoc (arXiv Sep 2026, listed as ECCV 2026). "Foldformer" and "SalmRec" could not be found and should be treated as non-existent names; no Gaussian-splat/NeRF page-flattening model was found.

### Inferences
- The field's 2025-2026 centre of gravity for UVDoc-like lightweight grid models is the Xi'an Jiaotong / U Tokyo group (AADD, ArbDR) and ETH (SyntheticDoc); all three keep UVDoc's architecture and grid and change loss, cascade or data.
- "UniDoc" as a dewarping model was not found; the Hertlein et al. line is Inv3D (dataset + template GeoTr) -> DocMatcher.

### Gaps
- "Foldformer" and "SalmRec": zero hits in two searches and in both curated lists; I believe these names do not correspond to real document dewarping papers (not verified exhaustively).
- "DocLine" / "LineAware" as model names: not found. The real line-aware models are DocTLNet, D2Dewarp, ForCenNet, AADD, DocMatcher, TextFlow.
- No Gaussian-splatting or NeRF based page flattening method was found; the only mention is ArbDR citing 3D Gaussian Splatting as future work (multi-view) — [ArbDR](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.23150).
- Venue of ForCenNet (ICCV 2025) and SyntheticDoc (ECCV 2026) rest on community lists only; arXiv pages do not state the venue.
- I did not find a 2024-2026 survey paper dedicated to dewarping, nor a live Papers-With-Code leaderboard.
- DocHFormer, DocMamba, DAA-Net, AFH-Net, ICASSP 2024 joint rectification, TextFlow: details not retrieved (paywalled Springer/IEEE/ScienceDirect/SSRN; no code).

### Inferences
- All benchmarks are text documents downscaled for evaluation; LD/AD averaged over a page barely register a localized kink at a crease, so none of these numbers directly measure the user's problem. AAD (axis-aligned straightness of feature lines) is the closest proxy; by AAD, ArbDR (0.085) and AADD (0.099) beat UVDoc (0.125) on DocUNet.
- ForCenNet's numbers are the best on paper, but it has no released code or weights (see below), so they are unreproducible at present.
- The single most relevant data point for grid coarseness: same UVDoc-style architecture and data, going from a 31x45 grid at 512x720 to a 121x177 grid at 1024x1440 improved DocUNet LD 5.98 -> 5.51 and AD 0.252 -> 0.219 (SyntheticDoc Table 5).

- No 2024-2026 model other than AADD was found reporting on the UVDoc benchmark.
- DvD DIR300/AnyPhotoDoc6300 numeric values, DocMatcher's numbers (Inv3DReal; CVF PDF returned 403), DocHFormer / DAA-Net / AFH-Net / DocMamba full numbers: not retrieved.
- DocTLNet numbers are second-hand (from TADoc and D2Dewarp tables), not from the IJDAR paper itself.

## Output type and applicability to a ~5000x7000 px original

### Takeaway
Every geometric model that matters here predicts coordinates (a coarse grid or a low-resolution backward map/flow), not pixels, so all can be upsampled and applied to a 5000x7000 original exactly like UVDoc; the pixel-generating exceptions to avoid are the unified diffusion restorers (Uni-DocDiff, the Stable-Diffusion-Transformer workshop paper). But all predict the warp at low resolution (288-720 px input, 31x45 to 64x64 effective grid), so the "coarse map smooths creases" limitation is shared; the only published denser variant is SyntheticDoc's 121x177 grid from a 1024x1440 input.

### Cited Findings
- UVDoc-lineage, coarse 45x31 grid from 712x488 input: AADD ("lightweight CNN", UVDoc architecture, predicts 2D grid plus 3D mesh) — [AADD](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.15000)
- ArbDR: three cascaded UVDoc-size networks, each a sparse 45x31 control grid at 712x488: L-Net (least-squares affine from the grid), C-Net (deformation grid), F-Net (iterative local grids composed into a cumulative backward map, adaptive stop, max 5 iterations); efficiency tested on 4096x3072 inputs — [ArbDR](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.23150)
- SyntheticDoc baseline: UVDoc architecture with the 3D head replaced by a U-Net shading head; 512x720 input -> 31x45 backward-map grid; high-resolution variant 1024x1440 input -> 121x177 grid — [SyntheticDoc](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.15503v1)
- TADoc: backward warping flow as sparse grid at H/16 x W/16 x 2, bilinearly interpolated; diffusion-like time-conditioned progressive process with T = 20 steps — [TADoc](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.06988)
- DvD: diffusion over a 64x64 latent coordinate mapping, upsampled to a full-resolution mapping; "coordinate-level denoising instead of typical pixel-level denoising"; paper cites 2000x3000 as a typical high-res input; 16x16 latent performs poorly "because it loses too much warping information" — [DvD](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.21975v2)
- ForCenNet: backward map predicted from 288x288 input (features at H/8), upsampled to high resolution — [ForCenNet](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.19804v1)
- D2Dewarp: dense 2-channel deformation field; training at 448x448; also predicts horizontal and vertical line masks — [D2Dewarp](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.08492)
- DocRes: dewarping = "predicting the backward map" (coordinates, not pixels), dewarping images resized to 256x256 in training; uses a document mask from an external segmentation model (Marior) as prior — [DocRes](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.04408)
- DocMatcher: template-based; detects structural and textual lines in a pre-unwarped image and matches them to lines of a known flat template; the repo's template models need a `template_` image per input — [doc-matcher repo](https://github.com/FelixHertlein/doc-matcher), [search summary of WACV paper](https://www.openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2025/papers/Hertlein_DocMatcher_Document_Image_Dewarping_via_Structural_and_Textual_Line_Matching_WACV_2025_paper.pdf)
- Uni-DocDiff: diffusion-based unified restoration model (abstract does not state task list or output format) — [arXiv 2508.04055](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.04055)
- DvD authors note GPT-4o style pixel generation yields clean-looking but "chaotic" content for lack of explicit deformation awareness — [DvD](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.21975v2)

### Inferences
- FLAG (pixel-generating, cannot be applied to a high-res original and may hallucinate drawing content): Uni-DocDiff and "Document Image Rectification using Stable Diffusion Transformer" are presumed image-space diffusion models; this is inferred from titles/abstract, not verified.
- DvD and TADoc are "diffusion-style" but output coordinates, so they are safe to apply at full resolution.
- DocMatcher is unusable for a one-off A2 floor plan: no flat digital template exists.
- A 45x31 grid over an A2 sheet has roughly 13-19 mm node spacing; a crease is a C1 discontinuity narrower than one cell, so any bilinear/bicubic upsampling of such a grid will round it. ArbDR's iterative F-Net composes several coarse grids, which sharpens residual correction but each step is still 45x31. The 121x177 grid (about 4x denser per axis) is the only published neural output with noticeably finer spatial support.

### Gaps
- DocScanner's input size and BM resolution are not stated in the repo README (from memory it is 288x288; unverified).
- DocTLNet output representation not retrieved.
- Whether D2Dewarp/ForCenNet inference code handles very large originals efficiently is undocumented.

### Inferences
- CPU realistic: AADD (same cost as UVDoc), SyntheticDoc 512x720 baseline (similar), DocScanner, TADoc (20 steps x a 7.9M net; slower but feasible), ArbDR (roughly 3-8x UVDoc cost). The SyntheticDoc 1024x1440 variant is roughly 4x the pixels of UVDoc's input, so likely a few seconds on CPU extrapolating from PaddleOCR's 0.87 s UVDoc CPU figure (estimate, not measured).

### Gaps
- No paper reports CPU timings; the only CPU figure is PaddleOCR's for UVDoc.
- ForCenNet parameter count and speed are not published in the arXiv version.

## Evidence about folds and creases, line-straightness constraints, residual kinks

### Takeaway
No 2024-2026 neural model explicitly models creases or piecewise-planar surfaces, and no paper demonstrates crease-free straight lines on folded paper; the nearest things are straight-line/axis-alignment losses (AADD, ArbDR, ForCenNet, D2Dewarp), iterative local refinement (ArbDR F-Net), and crease-rich simulated training data with a denser grid (SyntheticDoc). ArbDR openly states severe folds are not fully flattened.

### Cited Findings
Line/straightness constraints:
- AADD: axis-aligned loss L_AL in UV space = sum of row-wise variances of v + column-wise variances of u (weight 0.2), premised on feature lines (text lines, table lines) being horizontal/vertical in the flat page; new AAD metric measures gradient-weighted row/column deviation of SIFT-flow; AAD improves 18.2% (DocUNet) to 34.5% (DIR300) over prior SOTA. The paper "does not discuss folds or creases in its method or experiments" (creased-document work appears only in related work) — [AADD](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.15000)
- ArbDR: axis-alignment loss "enforce[s] the straightness of feature lines"; F-Net handles "subtle, content-level distortions, such as warped text lines"; adaptive stopping uses LSD-detected line segments within 5 degrees of an axis; fixed iteration counts beyond optimum increase AAD (over-correction) — [ArbDR](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.23150)
- ForCenNet: straight lines detected with LSD (near-horizontal/vertical only) form foreground labels with text; "curvature consistency loss" samples points every 4 px along undistorted line elements and compares curvature after mapping through predicted vs ground-truth BM; removing it changes MS 0.571 -> 0.565 and CER 0.141 -> 0.169; claims the mask module "effectively handles complex distortions, such as folds, creases, and shadows" (qualitative only); line-straightness check by total detected line length beats DocRes on 65% of DocReal and 69% of WarpDoc samples — [ForCenNet](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.19804v1)
- D2Dewarp: supervises horizontal-line and vertical-line masks (edges of document, tables, figures, paragraphs, text lines) with BCE plus RDGR's line loss; new DocDewarpHV dataset; does not mention folds or creases; limitation: text-sparse documents give lines only along boundaries and charts, weaker guidance — [D2Dewarp](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.08492)
- DocMatcher: matches structural and textual lines to a template — [doc-matcher repo](https://github.com/FelixHertlein/doc-matcher)
- TextFlow: text-line-guided auxiliary objective (unwarped text map); no mention of folds or line constraints — [TextFlow repo](https://github.com/Liu-Shaokai/TextFlow)

Fold-specific evidence and honest limitations:
- ArbDR limitations: severe, complex folds (e.g. WarpDoc "random" category) are not fully flattened — readability improves but the result is not perfectly flat; causes given are scarce training data with high-frequency distortions and the inherent ambiguity of single-view reconstruction; future work proposes multi-view 3D modelling such as 3D Gaussian Splatting — [ArbDR](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.23150)
- SyntheticDoc training data explicitly simulates creases with the ArcSim physics simulator on A4 sheets: a "Fold" procedure (pull one boundary vertex over another, roll a cylinder to form a crease, unfold; "convincing geometries with one fold"), "fall on gable" (sharper fold-like deformations), "fall on multiple spheres" (many creases, heavily crumpled); 1,000,000 training samples at 1024x1440; no straight-line loss, no discussion of grid-resolution limits beyond the 31x45 vs 121x177 ablation — [SyntheticDoc](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.15503v1)
- DvD: benchmark AnyPhotoDoc6300 evaluates folds, curves and crumples separately; claims "precise structure preservation"; creases and straight lines not discussed; limitations: gains negligible on unseen document types (invoices, educational scripts), trained on Doc3D only — [DvD](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.21975v2)
- TADoc: no fold-specific method; folding only appears among qualitative deformation types ("rotation, bending, and folding"); says previous methods "struggle with subtle deformation at the edge or corners" — [TADoc](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.06988)
- DocRes: no mention of folds/creases — [DocRes](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.04408)
- Older observation still cited: methods fail when curves and folds occur simultaneously (RectiNet-v2) — [arXiv 2102.01120](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.01120)
- Crease-explicit work exists only outside the neural 2024-2026 set: Unfolder (single crease from folding in half, projective model per half) and Luo & Bo's isometric-mapping method for creased documents — [Unfolder](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00467), [Luo & Bo](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.08365)

### Inferences
- For a floor plan dominated by long axis-parallel straight lines and a rectangular frame, AADD/ArbDR's premise (feature lines should be axis-aligned) matches the content far better than text-line methods (DocTLNet, TextFlow, D2Dewarp), which depend on text density.
- AADD's axis-aligned loss penalises variance along whole grid rows/columns, so it reduces global bow and waviness, but with the same 45x31 grid it cannot represent a sharp crease any better than UVDoc; expect smaller but still visible kinks. ArbDR's iterative local refinement is the neural approach most likely to reduce residual kinks, and its own authors say folds remain imperfect.
- SyntheticDoc's hi-res (121x177) model is the most promising direction specifically for creases: it is trained on physically simulated creases and has ~4x finer grid spacing. This is an inference; the paper shows no crease-specific evaluation.
- Because no learned model guarantees straightness, a practical remedy probably remains a post-hoc, content-driven refinement (detect long line segments in the UVDoc output and solve for a residual warp that straightens them, or a piecewise-planar model split at detected crease lines) — consistent with ArbDR's LSD-based stopping criterion and the classical Unfolder approach. This is my inference, not a published result.

### Gaps
- No paper reports a quantitative "kink at crease" or line-straightness metric on folded documents other than AAD (page-level average) and ForCenNet's line-length comparison.
- No evaluation on large-format technical drawings (A2 plans) exists in any paper found; all benchmarks are A4-like text documents, receipts, books.
- ForCenNet's fold/crease claim is qualitative and unverifiable without released weights.
- I could not read ArbDR's WarpDoc tables or DvD's per-category (fold/curve/crumple) numbers.
