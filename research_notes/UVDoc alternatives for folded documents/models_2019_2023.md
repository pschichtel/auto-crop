## 1. Reported accuracy on DocUNet benchmark, DIR300 and UVDoc benchmark (MS-SSIM, LD, AD, CER/ED)

### Takeaway
On the DocUNet benchmark UVDoc has the best LD (6.83) and AD (0.315) of all 2019-2023 models in its own table, with 8M parameters; no 2019-2023 model beats it there on geometric metrics. On DIR300 (which is 40% folded documents) the picture is mixed: per second-hand tables UVDoc scores MS-SSIM 0.621 / LD 7.73 / AD 0.217, i.e. worse LD than DocGeoNet (6.40) but better AD (0.217 vs 0.242); only LA-DocFlatten (2023) is clearly ahead on both. Numbers differ between papers by a few percent because of benchmark variant (130 images with two wrongly rotated, 130 corrected, or 128), MATLAB/Tesseract versions and OCR subset (50 vs 60 images), so cross-table comparison is only approximate.

### Cited Findings

**Benchmark versions**

### Inferences
- Within 2019-2023, the only models that match or beat UVDoc on DocUNet geometric metrics (LD/AD) are LA-DocFlatten (AD about 0.30-0.31, LD 6.72-7.24 depending on the citing paper) and DocReal (AD 0.284, LD 7.00, published Jan 2024). DocTr, DocGeoNet, DocScanner, PaperEdge, Marior, RDGR, FDRNet, DocTr++ are all worse than UVDoc on LD and AD.
- FDRNet's high MS-SSIM (0.543) with mediocre LD/AD (8.08/0.396) supports DocGeoNet's remark that MS-SSIM is a poor measure of geometric accuracy; LD/AD and UVDoc's line-straightness metric are the relevant ones for "straight lines stay straight".
- UVDoc's lead on its own benchmark is partly home advantage (benchmark produced by the same capture pipeline as its training data), but the line-straightness columns (1.82/2.48 vs 2.24/3.91 for DocGeoNet) are the only published straight-line metric comparing these models.

### Gaps
- No direct paper-reported DIR300 or WarpDoc numbers for UVDoc from its own authors; the DIR300/WarpDoc/DocReal UVDoc rows come from ForCenNet via an automated summariser and should be verified against the ForCenNet PDF.
- DIR300 numbers for DocScanner, Marior, RDGR, FDRNet, DocTr++ were not found in the sources read.
- LA-DocFlatten (Li et al., ACM TOG 2023, "Layout-aware single-image document flattening") was not read directly; only its scores as quoted by later papers are given, and those quotes disagree.
- No benchmark reports metrics on a fold-only subset for any of these models in the main papers (see section 4).

## 2. What each network outputs, and can it be applied to a 5000x7000 px image?

### Takeaway
Every 2019-2023 model predicts a low-resolution warp (a backward map of 64x64 to 288x288, or a 9x9 to 45x31 control grid) from a heavily downsampled input, and then upsamples that warp to the photo resolution. All can therefore be applied to a 5000x7000 image in principle, but none operates at a resolution where a crease is sharper than several source pixels; the "dense flow" transformer family (DocTr, DocGeoNet, DocScanner, DocTr++) actually regresses at 1/8 of a 288x288 input, i.e. a 36x36 lattice, which is coarser than UVDoc's 45x31 grid.

### Cited Findings
- UVDoc: fully convolutional encoder, two heads predicting a 45x31 2D unwarping grid (pixel coords in [-1,1], a coarse backward map) and a 45x31 3D grid (training-only regulariser) from a 488x712 input; the 2D grid is bilinearly interpolated to the original image size and used to resample the full-resolution photo — [UVDoc paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.02887)
- DewarpNet: shape network predicts per-pixel 3D world coordinates, texture-mapping network predicts a dense backward map B (h x w x 2); bilinear sampling of the input — [DewarpNet](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_ICCV_2019/papers/Das_DewarpNet_Single-Image_Document_Unwarping_With_Stacked_3D_and_2D_Regression_ICCV_2019_paper.pdf). In the released inference code the input is resized to 256x256, the backward map is predicted at 128x128, box-blurred with a 3x3 kernel, then resized to the original image size — [DewarpNet infer.py](https://github.com/cvlab-stonybrook/DewarpNet/blob/master/infer.py)
- DocTr (GeoTr): input resized to 288x288; transformer decoder features at 1/8 resolution; a learned upsampling "tail" produces the backward mapping at 288x288; a preceding segmentation network removes background — [DocTr](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.12942). Applied to high-res photos by resizing the flow (as described for the same pipeline in DocTr++, below).
- DocGeoNet: predicts a coarse displacement map at (H/8 x W/8) with a two-layer conv head, then upsamples to full H x W "by taking learnable weighted combination of the 3x3 grid of the coarse resolution neighbors of each pixel" (RAFT-style convex upsampling); ablation: learnable vs bilinear upsampling 0.5040/7.71 vs 0.5062/7.77 (MS-SSIM/LD), i.e. a small difference — [DocGeoNet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.08161)
- DocScanner: segmentation (document localisation) module, then a recurrent ConvGRU refiner that iteratively (K=12) updates a warping flow at 1/8 resolution (feature maps 36x36, so a 288x288 working resolution), with learnable upsampling to H x W; the authors note "the coarse bilinear upsampling operation ... likely can not recover the small deformations of the distorted document" — [DocScanner](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.14968)
- DocTr++: "the real-world distorted document images of arbitrary resolutions are first resized to (288, 288) ... the network ... produces a warping flow of shape (288, 288). Finally, we resize the flow map to the original shape of the input distorted image and unwarp it with the obtained flow map"; handles photos with partial or no document boundary — [DocTr++](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08796)
- PaperEdge: two networks. Enet (global, boundary-based) takes 256x256x5 (RGB + coordinate channels) and outputs a 64x64x2 backward deformation field; Tnet (local, texture-based) takes the Enet-unwarped image and outputs a second deformation field that is composed with the first — [PaperEdge](https://zhixinshu.github.io/pdf/SIGGRAPH_22_PAPEREDGE.pdf)
- FDRNet: predicts control points of a mesh grid for a thin-plate-spline (TPS) warp via coarse + refinement transformers; "a 9x9 mesh grid (with 81 control points) is sufficient"; high-frequency (Fourier) components used for the loss — [FDRNet](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2022/papers/Xue_Fourier_Document_Restoration_for_Robust_Document_Dewarping_and_Recognition_CVPR_2022_paper.pdf)
- RDGR: not end-to-end. Networks detect document boundary (DocUNet at 128x128) and text lines; an energy minimisation with a grid-regularisation term then solves for the deformation grid. "the size of the deformation grid unit is currently set to be 128 x 128, which somewhat limits the density of the discrete geometric" constraints — [RDGR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.16850)
- Marior: margin-removal module (mask + corner/edge control points + TPS; skipped when edges are incomplete), then an iterative content rectification module that predicts a dense displacement flow "that has the same resolution as" its input, applied repeatedly with an adaptive stopping rule — [Marior](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.11515)
- DDCP: predicts 31x31 control points plus 31x31 reference points; dense map obtained by TPS or linear interpolation; explicitly designed so the control points can be rescaled to rectify at any output resolution; input is scaled to 992x992 — [DDCP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.10543)
- DocReal: PaperEdge's Enet for coarse dewarp, then an attention-enhanced control-point network predicting a 31x31 grid from a 992x992 input — [DocReal](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2024/papers/Yu_DocReal_Robust_Document_Dewarping_of_Real-Life_Images_via_Attention-Enhanced_Control_WACV_2024_paper.pdf)
- PWUNet (piece-wise unwarping): shape network at 256x256, then local backward maps regressed on n x n patches (n=2, 128x128 patches) and stitched by a learned module into a global backward map — [PWUNet](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2021/papers/Das_End-to-End_Piece-Wise_Unwarping_of_Document_Images_ICCV_2021_paper.pdf)
- Polar-Doc: one-stage control-point regression in polar coordinates with joint segmentation, grid-format control points, 9.6M params — [Polar-Doc](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.07925)
- DocMAE: masked-autoencoder pre-training, 288x288 input, patch size 16 — [DocMAE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.10341)
- DocAligner is not a single-image dewarper: it predicts a dense correspondence flow between a photographed document and its clean digital counterpart (hierarchical global/local correlation plus high-resolution refinement) in order to auto-annotate real photos — [DocAligner](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05749)

### Inferences
- Sampling density on a 5000x7000 photo: UVDoc's 45x31 grid gives one node per roughly 160 px; a 36x36 (1/8 of 288) regression lattice gives one independent estimate per roughly 140-195 px; a 288x288 upsampled flow has one value per roughly 17-24 px but its information content is still the 36x36 lattice plus whatever sharpness the learned convex upsampler adds; PaperEdge's Enet 64x64 gives one per roughly 80-110 px; FDRNet 9x9 TPS and DDCP/DocReal 31x31 are coarser than or similar to UVDoc. A sharp crease on an A2 sheet is a discontinuity in the warp's first derivative that is 1-3 px wide at photo resolution, so none of these representations can localise it; each will spread the kink over at least one grid/lattice cell.
- Learned convex upsampling (DocTr/DocGeoNet/DocScanner) can in principle preserve a sharper edge than bilinear/TPS interpolation, but the papers' own ablations show the benefit is marginal on benchmark metrics, and the input the network sees (288x288) is lower-resolution than UVDoc's 488x712, so a fine crease on a large drawing is even less visible to those networks.
- UVDoc's representation is the easiest to post-process for the fold problem (a small regular grid that can be refined or re-fitted piecewise), whereas dense-flow models would hand over a 288x288 field with the same smoothing already baked in.
- Models that need a visible full document boundary and a prior segmentation step (DocTr, DocGeoNet, DocScanner, PaperEdge-Enet, DocReal) match the user's case (whole sheet on a wooden floor), so that is not a blocker; background similarity is a stated failure mode only for FDRNet.

### Gaps
- Tnet's output resolution in PaperEdge and Marior's working resolution were not confirmed from the text read.
- Whether any released inference script applies the flow at full photo resolution without an intermediate resize (other than as described for DocTr++/DewarpNet) was not checked per repository (deployment is covered by another researcher).

## 3. Input resolution, parameter count / model size, inference time

### Takeaway
UVDoc (8M parameters, 488x712 input) is among the smallest and has the highest input resolution of the purely learned models except the 992x992 control-point models (DDCP, DocReal); only DocScanner-T/B (2.6M/5.2M) are smaller. Published timings are all GPU frame rates on a 1080p image; no CPU inference times were found for any of these models.

### Cited Findings
- Parameter counts: DewarpNet 86.9M; DispFlow 23.6M; DocTr 26.9M; DDCP 13.3M; PaperEdge 36.6M; DocGeoNet 24.8M; UVDoc 8M; FDRNet, RDGR, Marior, PW Unwarping not reported ("-") — [UVDoc paper Table 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.02887)
- PaperEdge's own paper says 36.4M (and a slim 14.8M ablation variant); each released checkpoint (Enet, Tnet) is 139.7 MB — [PaperEdge](https://zhixinshu.github.io/pdf/SIGGRAPH_22_PAPEREDGE.pdf), [PaperEdge repo](https://github.com/cvlab-stonybrook/PaperEdge)
- DocScanner-T / -B / -L: 2.6M / 5.2M / 8.5M parameters, 10.81 / 10.03 / 9.52 FPS on a 1080P image on an RTX 2080Ti; DocScanner-B breakdown: localisation 0.014 s + progressive rectification 0.085 s = 0.099 s; same table: DewarpNet 8.17 FPS, DocTr 7.74, DDCP 14.09, DocGeoNet 8.57, PaperEdge 13.95, FCN-based 1.49 — [DocScanner](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.14968)
- DocTr++: 26.6M parameters, 11.29 FPS (DocTr 7.40, DocGeoNet 7.82, DewarpNet 7.14 in the same table) — [DocTr++](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08796)
- DewarpNet: "takes 32ms on average to process a 4K resolution image" (GPU; hardware not stated in the passage read) — [DewarpNet](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_ICCV_2019/papers/Das_DewarpNet_Single-Image_Document_Unwarping_With_Stacked_3D_and_2D_Regression_ICCV_2019_paper.pdf)
- DDCP: 25 ms with linear interpolation of 31x31 points, 330 ms with TPS on 11x16 points, 61 ms TPS 4x6 — [DDCP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.10543)
- Polar-Doc 9.6M; Inv3D (GeoTr-based, template-conditioned) 25.7M — [Polar-Doc](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.07925)
- Input resolutions: UVDoc 488x712 — [UVDoc paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.02887); DewarpNet 256x256 (backward map 128x128) — [DewarpNet infer.py](https://github.com/cvlab-stonybrook/DewarpNet/blob/master/infer.py); DocTr 288x288 — [DocTr](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.12942); DocTr++ 288x288 — [DocTr++](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08796); DocScanner 288x288 (36x36 features at 1/8) — [DocScanner](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.14968); PaperEdge 256x256 — [PaperEdge](https://zhixinshu.github.io/pdf/SIGGRAPH_22_PAPEREDGE.pdf); DDCP 992x992 — [DDCP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.10543); DocReal 992x992 — [DocReal](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2024/papers/Yu_DocReal_Robust_Document_Dewarping_of_Real-Life_Images_via_Attention-Enhanced_Control_WACV_2024_paper.pdf); DocMAE 288x288 — [DocMAE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.10341); RDGR boundary network trained at 128x128 — [RDGR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.16850)
- RDGR's optimisation step is described by its authors as computationally costly (reason the grid is limited to 128x128) and the pipeline is not end-to-end — [RDGR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.16850)
- Marior is iterative ("More iterations consume more time and even introduce new distortions"), with an adaptive stop — [Marior](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.11515)

### Inferences
- All the transformer-based alternatives (DocTr, DocGeoNet, DocTr++) are 3x larger than UVDoc and additionally need a segmentation pre-network; DocScanner-L is the only alternative with a comparable footprint (8.5M) and it is iterative (12 ConvGRU steps).
- Since UVDoc is plain convolutions on a 488x712 input, it is likely the cheapest of the accurate models on CPU, but this is not backed by a published CPU benchmark.

### Gaps
- No CPU inference times were found in any of the papers read; UVDoc's paper gives no timing at all, only the parameter count and the claim that the coarse grid makes it "very efficient".
- DocGeoNet's input resolution was not confirmed from its text (its sibling models DocTr/DocScanner/DocTr++ use 288x288; DocGeoNet's 1/8-scale design is consistent with that but the number was not located).
- FDRNet, RDGR, Marior parameter counts and timings are not reported in any table read.

## 4. Folds / creases versus smooth curls: what the papers and independent evaluations say

### Takeaway
No source read shows any 2019-2023 model rectifying sharp fold lines better than UVDoc, and no paper reports a fold-only quantitative comparison that includes UVDoc. Several papers concede that creases and crumples are the weak spot, and the dominant training set (Doc3D) contains no fine crease geometry at all. The evidence points to residual kinks at creases being a shared limitation of low-resolution warp regression, not a UVDoc-specific defect.

### Cited Findings
- Doc3D lacks crease detail by construction: meshes were captured with an inexpensive depth sensor and subsampled to a 100x100 uniform grid; "even a higher resolution mesh grid cannot provide finer details like subtle creases", and under Limitations: "the inexpensive depth sensor cannot capture fine details of deformation like subtle creases on a paper crumple. Thus our data lacks samples with highly complex paper crumple" — [DewarpNet](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_ICCV_2019/papers/Das_DewarpNet_Single-Image_Document_Unwarping_With_Stacked_3D_and_2D_Regression_ICCV_2019_paper.pdf)
- DewarpNet is the only paper read with a per-deformation-class evaluation on DocUNet: classes (a) perspective, (b) curled, (c) one-fold "one significant crease", (d) multi-fold, (e) random-easy, (f) random-hard; DewarpNet improves on DocUNet (2018) in classes (a)-(e) and is only "comparable and slightly better" on (f). Values are given only as a bar chart (Fig. 6), and only against DocUNet — [DewarpNet](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_ICCV_2019/papers/Das_DewarpNet_Single-Image_Document_Unwarping_With_Stacked_3D_and_2D_Regression_ICCV_2019_paper.pdf)
- UVDoc's dataset was made by physically folding and bending paper ("curved, folded, and crumpled") and recording an 89x61 dot grid; its ground truth is "more physically accurate (albeit coarser), since we do not apply any smoothing to it". The method assumes the sheet is a height field with no fold-overs, and predicts a coarse grid "in an attempt to keep our network as compact as possible". The paper has no limitations section on creases; the only stated residual issue is shape-from-shading making results "appear more distorted to the human eye than they geometrically are" — [UVDoc paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.02887)
- UVDoc ranks best on horizontal/vertical line straightness on its benchmark (1.82 / 2.48) vs DocTr 2.42 / 3.32, DocGeoNet 2.24 / 3.91, DewarpNet 3.22 / 4.32, DDCP 4.20 / 4.88, RDGR 4.03 / 5.87 — [UVDoc paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.02887)
- FDRNet argues that per-pixel regression suffers from imbalance: "only a small portion of pixels exhibit irregular deformations (e.g. pixels around crumples)" leading to "degraded performance for existing pixel-level regression-based models while handling documents with irregular deformations"; its answer is a *coarser* model (9x9 TPS mesh) that "is less affected by the pixel-level data imbalance" — i.e. robustness, not crease sharpness — [FDRNet](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2022/papers/Xue_Fourier_Document_Restoration_for_Robust_Document_Dewarping_and_Recognition_CVPR_2022_paper.pdf)
- WarpDoc has an explicit "Fold" category (one or several creases) among six, but FDRNet's main paper reports only aggregate WarpDoc scores; per-category details are deferred to its supplement — [FDRNet](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2022/papers/Xue_Fourier_Document_Restoration_for_Robust_Document_Dewarping_and_Recognition_CVPR_2022_paper.pdf)
- PaperEdge: "synthesizing hyper-realistic warped, creased, and crumpled document paper is very challenging"; real DIW training images are "especially beneficial for hard-to-synthesis document deformation types" (categorical analysis in its supplement); limitations: "3D agnostic ... The unwarping result is not guaranteed to be physically correct. PaperEdge also failed to handle some complex crumpled paper cases" — [PaperEdge](https://zhixinshu.github.io/pdf/SIGGRAPH_22_PAPEREDGE.pdf)
- PWUNet motivates piece-wise unwarping with folds: "a complexly folded/warped document is a combination of multiple simpler deformations"; low-dimensional parametric models "cannot model very complex surface deformation with multiple folds"; earlier work by the same group used segmentation "to detect the fold lines and divide" the document. But its benchmark scores (0.490 / LD 8.65 / AD 0.431 in UVDoc's table) are worse than UVDoc's — [PWUNet](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2021/papers/Das_End-to-End_Piece-Wise_Unwarping_of_Document_Images_ICCV_2021_paper.pdf), [UVDoc paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.02887)
- DocScanner and DocGeoNet both note that coarse bilinear upsampling of the 1/8-scale flow "likely can not recover the small deformations of the distorted document", motivating learnable upsampling; the measured gain is small (DocScanner-B: bilinear 0.5072 / LD 8.03 vs full model 0.5134 / 7.62; DocGeoNet 7.77 vs 7.71 LD). Their stated limitations concern missing boundaries and large backgrounds, not creases — [DocScanner](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.14968), [DocGeoNet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.08161)
- RDGR: in the interior "our dewarping results are more smooth than DocUNet and DewarpNet as we did not use the predicted deformation field in the interior regions" but solve for it with a smoothness-regularised grid optimisation driven by text lines and boundaries — [RDGR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.16850)
- DIR300 (40% folds, 10% crumpled): DocGeoNet LD 6.40 vs DocTr 7.21 vs DewarpNet 13.94 — [DocGeoNet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.08161); second-hand UVDoc on DIR300 LD 7.73 / AD 0.217 — [ForCenNet](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.19804v1)
- DocReal benchmark has a "Folded: visible creases" class (12%) and a per-type robustness plot (Fig. 7); the text only quantifies gains over DDCP (folded: +21% MS-SSIM, -40% LD); DocReal adds synthetic "corner folded" and "edge folded" augmentations because Doc3D's deformation range was judged insufficient — [DocReal](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2024/papers/Yu_DocReal_Robust_Document_Dewarping_of_Real-Life_Images_via_Attention-Enhanced_Control_WACV_2024_paper.pdf)
- A later paper (DvD, 2025) still describes existing regression methods as "resulting in imprecise structural preservation" and observes "a notable MS-SSIM drop for dark lighting documents with folds and crumples" — second-hand — [DvD](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.21975v1)

### Inferences
- Because (a) Doc3D ground truth is smoothed/low-resolution and has no crease detail, (b) every network sees the page at 256-288 px (UVDoc: 488x712) and (c) every output is a smooth interpolation of a lattice of at most about 45 nodes per side (or a TPS, which is smooth by definition), all 2019-2023 models should be expected to leave residual kinks at sharp folds on a large-format drawing. Swapping UVDoc for DocTr, DocGeoNet, DocScanner, PaperEdge, FDRNet, RDGR, Marior or DocTr++ is unlikely to remove them, and on benchmark LD/AD and line straightness it would more likely be a regression.
- UVDoc is arguably the *best*-positioned of the group for creases: it is the only one trained on physically folded real paper with unsmoothed ground truth and the only one evaluated on a line-straightness metric. Its remaining error at creases is a resolution limit (45x31 nodes, bilinear interpolation), not a training-data gap.
- Text-line-driven methods (RDGR, DocGeoNet, FTA) get their fine-scale signal from text lines; a floor plan with long ruled lines and little text gives them less to work with, and their text-line detectors are not trained on drawings. This is an inference; no paper tests technical drawings.
- The two candidates that could plausibly be better on folds are DIR300-leading LA-DocFlatten (2023) and DocReal (explicit fold augmentation, 31x31 control points from a 992x992 input); neither has released evidence specific to sharp creases, and DocReal states its source code cannot currently be provided — [DocReal](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2024/papers/Yu_DocReal_Robust_Document_Dewarping_of_Real-Life_Images_via_Attention-Enhanced_Control_WACV_2024_paper.pdf).

### Gaps
- No independent evaluation was found that compares these models specifically on sharp fold lines, on large-format drawings, or with a line-straightness metric on real folded documents.
- Supplementary materials with per-category results (PaperEdge categorical analysis, FDRNet WarpDoc per-type, DocReal Sec. D numbers, DewarpNet Fig. 6 bar values) were not retrieved; they would be the closest thing to fold-subset numbers, but none includes UVDoc.
- UVDoc's supplementary figures (all DocUNet/UVDoc-benchmark outputs) were not inspected for visible crease kinks.
- LA-DocFlatten's handling of folds was not checked (paper not read).

## 5. Training data

### Takeaway
Almost everything from 2019-2023 is trained on Doc3D (100k synthetic renders with smoothed, crease-free geometry). The exceptions are PaperEdge (adds 5,000 real DIW photos with weak boundary supervision), FDRNet (about 1k real images, no deformation ground truth) and UVDoc (Doc3D plus 20k pseudo-photorealistic images of physically folded paper).

### Cited Findings
- Doc3D: 100,000 rendered images from real captured 3D meshes (100x100 grid) with 3D coordinate, depth, normal, UV and backward-map ground truth — [DewarpNet](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_ICCV_2019/papers/Das_DewarpNet_Single-Image_Document_Unwarping_With_Stacked_3D_and_2D_Regression_ICCV_2019_paper.pdf)
- DewarpNet: Doc3D — [DewarpNet](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_ICCV_2019/papers/Das_DewarpNet_Single-Image_Document_Unwarping_With_Stacked_3D_and_2D_Regression_ICCV_2019_paper.pdf)
- DocTr: geometric transformer and segmentation trained on Doc3D (illumination module on DRIC); notes the Doc3D image size limit of 448x448 — [DocTr](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.12942)
- DocScanner: Doc3D — [DocScanner](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.14968)
- PWUNet: Doc3D — [PWUNet](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2021/papers/Das_End-to-End_Piece-Wise_Unwarping_of_Document_Images_ICCV_2021_paper.pdf)
- Marior: both modules trained on Doc3D — [Marior](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.11515)
- RDGR: boundary network trained on Doc3D; text-line UNet trained separately — [RDGR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.16850)
- FTA: Doc3D, 100,000 images — [FTA](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2023/papers/Li_Foreground_and_Text-lines_Aware_Document_Image_Rectification_ICCV_2023_paper.pdf)
- DocMAE: self-supervised pre-training on its own LDIR set, fine-tuning on Doc3D — [DocMAE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.10341)
- PaperEdge: Doc3D (supervised) + DIW, 5,000 real photos with annotated boundary masks, resized to 512x512, used for weakly supervised boundary loss (Enet) and a siamese perturbation-consistency loss (Tnet); DIW improved AD from 0.416 to 0.392 — [PaperEdge](https://zhixinshu.github.io/pdf/SIGGRAPH_22_PAPEREDGE.pdf)
- FDRNet: about 1k real camera images without deformation ground truth (table row "FDRNet 1k, no D-GT, Real"; a 130-image variant is used for the WarpDoc experiment), trained through a mutual-transformation loss in Fourier high-frequency space — [FDRNet](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2022/papers/Xue_Fourier_Document_Restoration_for_Robust_Document_Dewarping_and_Recognition_CVPR_2022_paper.pdf)
- DDCP: its own synthetic control-point dataset (2D deformations) — [DDCP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.10543)
- DocReal: 2D synthetic images derived from Doc3D meshes by sampling 31x31 control points, plus formula-generated curled and folded (corner-folded, edge-folded) augmentations — [DocReal](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2024/papers/Yu_DocReal_Robust_Document_Dewarping_of_Real-Life_Images_via_Attention-Enhanced_Control_WACV_2024_paper.pdf)
- UVDoc: Doc3D + UVDoc dataset (20k pseudo-photorealistic samples; A4 sheets printed with an invisible UV-ink 89x61 dot grid, physically folded/bent, photographed under UV and normal light; ground-truth 2D grid, 3D grid and uv-parameterisation). Adding UVDoc data improved DocUNet MS-SSIM by 8.9%, LD by 12.9%, AD by 9.7% over Doc3D-only; fine-tuning DewarpNet with UVDoc data also improved it (DocUNet 0.475/8.40/0.411 to 0.504/7.68/0.400) — [UVDoc paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.02887)
- DocAligner's purpose is to generate training data: it aligns photos to their clean source to auto-label real images — [DocAligner](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05749)
- DocTr++ introduces its own UDIR dataset for unrestricted (partial/no-boundary) inputs — [DocTr++](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08796)

### Inferences
- The UVDoc dataset is model-agnostic and measurably improves a different architecture (DewarpNet), so a more promising route than switching models may be to keep UVDoc-style data but change the output resolution/representation; this is consistent with the Doc3D-only UVDoc ablation falling back to DocTr-level accuracy.
- None of the training sets contains technical drawings, large formats (all A4-like), or annotated sharp-crease ground truth at pixel accuracy (UVDoc's 89x61 dots on A4 sample the surface about every 3.3 mm).

### Gaps
- DocGeoNet's training set was not confirmed from the text read (expected Doc3D, as for the same authors' DocTr and DocScanner) — unverified.
- Exact construction of DocTr++'s UDIR training set (believed to be derived from Doc3D by cropping) and Polar-Doc's training mix (its related-work section mentions Doc3D and UVDoc) were not verified.
- Inv3D (Hertlein et al. 2023, template-conditioned, invoice-specific) and LA-DocFlatten training data were not researched; Inv3D needs a template of the flat document and scored poorly on DocUNet (0.443 / LD 9.32 / AD 0.512 per [Polar-Doc](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.07925)), so it is unlikely to be relevant.
